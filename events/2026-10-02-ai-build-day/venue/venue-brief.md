# Venue — North Dallas, Friday Oct 2, 1–4 PM

**Lock this Monday or Tuesday.** It's the long pole. Every listing says "North Dallas,
address to registrants," which buys you until Wednesday — but not past it.

> **A note on the named places below:** these are the categories and operators that fit,

## Requirements — what actually matters

Ranked. The first three are the ones that kill events.

| # | Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Power at every seat** | 20 laptops for 3 hours. You must be able to run power strips to every table. Ask specifically: "Can I run extension cords to the tables, or is that a fire code problem here?" Some venues say no. Find out Monday. |
| 2 | **Wifi that survives 20 simultaneous users** | Not "do you have wifi." Ask: "How many people have you had on it at once?" A guest network built for 5 people in a lobby will collapse. Get the password in advance. |
| 3 | **Flat-floor room with movable tables** | You need three clusters plus a front. A fixed-seating auditorium or a boardroom with one giant table makes the table format impossible. |
| 4 | **A screen or TV with HDMI** | For the cold open and the demos. Confirm the input type. Bring your own adapters regardless. |
| 5 | **Door access at 12:15** | You need 25 minutes before doors. Confirm who unlocks and get their cell number. |
| 6 | **Free or under ~$150** | You're not charging. Don't turn a free event into a cost center. |
| 7 | Whiteboard or wall space for flip chart | For the menu, the vote, the stuck list. Taping paper to a wall works — confirm they allow tape. |
| 8 | Easy, free, obvious parking | North Dallas — assume everyone drives. Free surface parking. If parking is complicated, you'll spend 1:00–1:10 texting directions instead of building. |
| 9 | Restrooms near the room | Ten-minute break for 20 people. |
| 10 | Outside food/drink allowed | Coffee and water minimum. |

## Best plays for a Friday four days out

### 1. A conference room in a business that wants to meet your audience ← start here
Title companies, commercial insurance brokers, commercial real estate offices, business
banks, and CPA firms in Plano / Addison / Frisco / Richardson all have conference rooms
that sit empty on Friday afternoons, and they all want relationships with local business
owners.

**The trade: ten minutes of the room, or just their logo on the listing, for the space.**
This is the highest-percentage call you can make on a Monday for a Friday, it's free,
the rooms are built for exactly this, and there's no booking bureaucracy — one person
can say yes.

Who to call: any title company or commercial insurance broker you've already worked with.
You're in commercial contracting — **you already know these people.** That's your unfair
advantage here and it beats every option below.

### 2. Coworking spaces
Common Desk, WeWork, Regus/Spaces, Venture X, and independent North Dallas coworking
operators all rent meeting rooms hourly, and most have a Legacy West / Plano / Addison /
Frisco presence. Typically $50–200 for three hours. Purpose-built: power, real wifi,
screens, movable furniture.

**Ask for the "event space" or "training room," not the "meeting room."** Meeting rooms
are boardroom-shaped. Some will comp or discount it if you say it's a free community
event and you'll credit them.

### 3. Public library meeting rooms
Plano, Richardson, Frisco, Addison and Dallas public libraries all have free meeting
rooms with power and projectors. **Genuinely free**, and usually a clean, quiet, no-
nonsense room.

Two catches: booking lead time is often 5–7 days (call anyway and ask about a
cancellation — Friday afternoons open up), and some systems restrict rooms for
commercial or promotional use. Read their policy. Free community event with no selling
usually qualifies; if you plan to pitch from the front, it doesn't.

### 4. Entrepreneur centers and startup hubs
The DEC Network, Capital Factory's Dallas location, university-affiliated innovation
centers, and chamber of commerce spaces. Mission-aligned, often free or cheap for a
community event, and they may promote it to their own list — which is real added
distribution in a week where you need it.

### 5. Restaurant or brewery private room
Last resort. They'll say yes fastest and they're easiest to book, but they're usually
loud, dim, short on outlets, and come with a food and beverage minimum. **Hands-on
building in a noisy restaurant room does not work.** Only if everything else falls
through, and only if you can see the power situation with your own eyes first.

## The call script

Call, don't email. Email on a Monday for a Friday is a lost week.

> Hi — my name's Yousef, I run a business here in DFW. I'm hosting a free workshop for
> local business owners this Friday afternoon, one to four, and I'm looking for a room
> for about twenty people with power and wifi.
>
> It's not a sales thing — everybody brings a laptop and we build something together.
> No selling from the front.
>
> Do you have anything Friday afternoon? And if it helps, I'm happy to credit you on
> the event listing, or give you ten minutes at the top to say who you are to the room.

Then the four questions that actually decide it:

1. **Can I run power strips to the tables, or is that a fire code issue for you?**
2. **How many people have you had on the wifi at once?**
3. **Are the tables movable — can I set up three clusters?**
4. **Can I get in at 12:15 to set up, and who unlocks the door?**

If they clear all four, book it on the call and get a confirmation in writing —
a text message counts.

## Where in "North Dallas"

If you want maximum turnout from owners and operators, the center of gravity is the
**Plano / Addison / Far North Dallas** corridor — roughly Dallas North Tollway and
Preston Road between 635 and 121. Frisco pulls well but adds 20 minutes for anyone
coming from inside the loop. Richardson and Addison are the easiest for the broadest
set of people to reach at 1 PM on a Friday.

**Say the nearest major cross streets in the Thursday text**, not just the address.
People navigate DFW by tollway exits.
