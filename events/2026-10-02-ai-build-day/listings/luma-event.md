## Registration questions — this is the whole show-rate strategy

You gave up price as a filter, so commitment has to come from somewhere else.
Make these **required**:

1. **What business do you own or run?** (short text, required)
2. **What do you want built by 4pm Friday?** (long text, required)
   Nobody writing a paragraph about their business ghosts you. This single question
   is worth more than a $25 ticket.
3. **Which build is closest to yours?** (multi-select, required — list all 8 from the menu)
   Doubles as your pre-vote. You walk in Friday already knowing which three tables to set up.
4. **Mobile number for the Thursday confirmation text** (required)
   Say plainly: "I text the address Thursday. No list, no marketing, just this event."

Then approve registrations manually. Approve anyone who answered #2 with a real
sentence. Anyone who wrote "idk" or "AI" gets a reply asking what they want built —
half will answer and become your best attendees.

### How it actually works

**The room votes.** I'm not deciding for you what's worth building. At 1:15 everybody votes, we take the **2–3 automations the most people actually need**, and we build them together — me working the room, you at a table with your laptop open.

**1:00** — I build one live in front of you. From nothing. Ten minutes. No slides. You see exactly where the bar is.
**1:15** — We vote. The top 2–3 win. You join the one closest to your business.
**1:30** — You write yours in one sentence: trigger, steps, output. This is the part everyone skips, and it's why most people's AI projects die in a browser tab.
**1:35** — Build.
**2:35** — Build. Hard rule: by 3:25 it runs end to end, even if it's ugly.
**3:25** — Everybody demos. Ninety seconds each. You run your thing in front of the room.
**3:50** — What breaks first, what to do Monday, how to hand it to your team.

That 3:25 demo is the whole design. Nobody finishes what they don't have to show.

---

### You don't need to be technical

This is the part people get wrong about events like this. **You are not writing code.** You are not learning a programming language.

What you actually need is three things: **a laptop, admin access to your own tools, and a paid ChatGPT or Claude account.** That's it. If you can log into your CRM and your email, you can build what we're building.

The people who struggle are never the non-technical ones — they're the ones who **can't get into their own systems.** So if your web guy set up your forms, or your phone system is controlled by a vendor, sort that out before Friday.

And if you've got someone who handles your marketing or your tech, **bring them.** This works better with two people from the same business: one who knows what's broken, one who knows where the logins are.

---

### What the room usually builds

These are the ones that come up most. You vote, the top few win — and if yours isn't here, put it on the form and we'll add it to the ballot.

- **A map of every decision maker in your market** — name, title, company, work email, direct mobile. In a spreadsheet you own and keep.
- **Missed call → text back in thirty seconds.** Highest return for the least work, if your phone rings and nobody catches it.
- **Inbound lead → a real, personalized reply in sixty seconds,** booked straight to your calendar. Not an autoresponder — an actual reply.
- **Notes in → finished, branded proposal out.** If quoting eats your Saturdays, this is yours.
- **A Monday 7am brief** on who in your market just hired, expanded, moved or raised money — and the line to open with.
- **Every Google review answered in your voice,** queued for your approval.
- **Talk into your phone after a site visit** → CRM note, follow-up email and next task, written for you.
- **Your numbers in → a plain-English answer** to "what is actually making me money."

Owners and operators of Dallas–Fort Worth service trade businesses. Contractors, commercial and home services, flooring, roofing, cleaning, mechanical, landscaping, and the B2B shops that sell to them.

- **Real data.** A customer list, your last five proposals, five replies you've actually sent to inbound leads, or a P&L export.
- **One number:** what a new customer is worth to you.

And come with one thing in your head: **the annoying task you do every single week that you shouldn't be doing.** That's your build.
