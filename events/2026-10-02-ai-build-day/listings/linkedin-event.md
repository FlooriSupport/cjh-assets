LIVE AND IN PERSON — Friday, October 2
1:00–4:00 PM CT · North Dallas, TX
Free · 20 seats · Bring a laptop

FOR DALLAS SERVICE TRADE OWNERS

Not a webinar. Not a panel. Not another talk about the future of AI.

You show up Friday afternoon with one real problem in your business. Three hours later you walk out with it automated and running. That's the whole event.

HOW IT ACTUALLY WORKS

The room votes. I'm not deciding for you what's worth building. At 1:15 everybody votes, we take the two or three automations the most people actually need, and we build them together — me working the room, you at a table with your laptop open.

1:00 — I build one live in front of you. From nothing. Ten minutes. No slides. You see exactly where the bar is.
1:15 — We vote. The top two or three win. You join the one closest to your business.
1:30 — You write yours in one sentence: trigger, steps, output. This is the part everyone skips, and it is why most people's AI projects die in a browser tab.
1:35 — Build.
2:35 — Build. Hard rule: by 3:25 it runs end to end, even if it is ugly.
3:25 — Everybody demos. Ninety seconds each. You run your thing in front of the room.
3:50 — What breaks first, what to do Monday, how to hand it to your team.

That 3:25 demo is the whole design. Nobody finishes what they don't have to show.

YOU DON'T NEED TO BE TECHNICAL

This is the part people get wrong about events like this. You are not writing code. You are not learning a programming language.

What you actually need is three things: a laptop, admin access to your own tools, and a paid ChatGPT or Claude account. That's it. If you can log into your CRM and your email, you can build what we're building.

The people who struggle are never the non-technical ones — they're the ones who can't get into their own systems. So if your web guy set up your forms, or your phone system is controlled by a vendor, sort that out before Friday.

And if you've got someone who handles your marketing or your tech, bring them. This works better with two people from the same business: one who knows what's broken, one who knows where the logins are.

WHAT THE ROOM USUALLY BUILDS

These come up most. You vote, the top few win — and if yours isn't here, put it on the registration form and we'll add it to the ballot.

• A map of every decision maker in your market — name, title, company, work email, direct mobile. In a spreadsheet you own and keep.
• Missed call, text back in thirty seconds. Highest return for the least work if your phone rings and nobody catches it.
• Inbound lead, a real personalized reply in sixty seconds, booked straight to your calendar. Not an autoresponder — an actual reply.
• Notes in, finished branded proposal out. If quoting eats your Saturdays, this is yours.
• A Monday 7am brief on who in your market just hired, expanded, moved or raised money — and the line to open with.
• Every Google review answered in your voice, queued for your approval.
• Talk into your phone after a site visit, get back the CRM note, the follow-up email and the next task.
• Your numbers in, a plain-English answer to "what is actually making me money."

WHO THIS IS FOR

Owners and operators of Dallas–Fort Worth service trade businesses. Contractors, commercial and home services, flooring, roofing, cleaning, mechanical, landscaping, and the B2B shops that sell to them. If you have customers, a phone that rings, and something you keep meaning to automate — you're who I built this for.

WHO THIS IS NOT FOR

Anyone who wants to watch. There are no observers in this room. Twenty seats, twenty people building.

WHAT TO BRING

A laptop. Not an iPad.
Your charger.
A paid ChatGPT or Claude account. The free tiers cut you off around minute forty.
Your logins — CRM, email, phone system. Bring your password manager. Every time I run one of these, somebody loses an hour to a password reset.
Real data. A customer list, your last five proposals, five replies you've sent to inbound leads, or a P&L export.
One number: what a new customer is worth to you.

And come with one thing in your head: the annoying task you do every single week that you shouldn't be doing. That's your build.

THE PRICE

It's free. The price is that you finish something and demo it. If you're not going to build, please leave the seat for someone who will.

WHO'S RUNNING IT

I'm Yousef Okasheh. I run Commercial Job Hunters here in DFW — we help skilled trade businesses win commercial and industrial contracts by replacing word-of-mouth with a proactive hunting system.

900+ businesses helped to grow profit. 6 years in commercial services. 5,955 decision makers mapped across Dallas–Fort Worth with verified work emails and direct mobiles.

I also founded Floori, a product visualizer used by flooring brands and retailers on three continents.

I'm not an AI guy who wandered into business. I'm a business guy who got tired of buying lists that were wrong, built the thing myself, and now does it for a living. Everything I'm showing you Friday is something I actually run inside my own companies.

## After you publish
1. Click **Invite** and invite connections — LinkedIn lets you invite in bulk.
   Filter by location: Dallas–Fort Worth. Do NOT blast all connections; LinkedIn
   throttles invites and DFW-only keeps the attendee list real.
2. Post the announcement post (`promo/linkedin-post.md`) **separately** and attach
   the event to it. The post is what actually travels; the event page just holds.
3. Comment on your own post with the Luma link about an hour after posting.
   LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound links in the body.
