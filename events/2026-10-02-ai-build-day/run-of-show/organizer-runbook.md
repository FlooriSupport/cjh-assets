# Organizer runbook — Friday, October 2

## The one thing to protect

**The 3:25 demo.** Everything else in this document exists to make sure that at 3:25,
every person in the room can run something in front of everyone else.

Nobody finishes what they don't have to show. If you cut anything for time, cut your own
talking. Do not cut the demos.

## The kit

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Power strips ×4 + extension cords | The #1 physical failure of a build event. Twenty laptops, three outlets. Bring more than you think. |
| HDMI cable + USB-C adapter + a spare of each | Venue adapters never work |
| Your own mobile hotspot | Venue wifi will be fine until 20 people hit it at once. Have a fallback for yourself at minimum. |
| Flip chart paper + easel, or big sticky pads | The menu, the vote, the stuck list |

## Run of show

### 12:15 — you arrive
Power strips down first. Tables into three clusters plus a front. Test the screen.
Write the eight menu items on flip chart paper. Tape the parking sign.

### 12:40 — doors
**This is setup time, not small talk time.** As each person walks in:
- Name tag
- On the wifi
- **Open ChatGPT or Claude in front of you and confirm they're logged into a paid account**
- Confirm the logins for whatever they're building

Anyone who shows up at 1:00 not logged into anything loses 25 minutes of build time.
Catching it at 12:40 is the difference between them finishing and not.

Have one thing for them to do while they wait: "write the one thing you want working
by 4 on this index card."

### 1:00–1:12 — cold open. You build one, live.
No introduction. No agenda slide. No "thanks for coming." Open your laptop and say:

> "Here's a thing that was broken in my business three weeks ago. Watch."

Build **missed-call text-back** or the **proposal generator** — whichever you can do
cold in ten minutes. Narrate what you're doing, not why it's impressive. Hit a small
error on purpose if one doesn't happen naturally, and fix it in front of them. That
error is worth more than the success: it shows them what being stuck looks like and
that it's survivable.

End on the demo. Call your own number. Let the room watch the text land.

Then: *"That's the bar. Everyone in here is doing that by 3:25."*

**Introduce yourself at 1:12, not 1:00.** Thirty seconds, after you've earned it.

### 1:12–1:20 — the vote
Walk the eight options, one line each. Three dot stickers per person. Count out loud.
Top three win. Assign tables.

### 1:20–1:35 — scope it. One sentence.
**The most valuable fifteen minutes of the afternoon.** Everyone writes on an index card:

```
WHEN ________________ happens,
   ________________ should happen,
so that I get ________________.
```

Then **every person reads theirs out loud to their table.** Not to the room — to their
table, 5–7 people, 90 seconds each.

Your job during this: circulate and cut scope. Ruthlessly. Almost everyone's first
version is three builds in a trench coat. "Every lead gets scored, routed, replied to,
and added to a nurture sequence" is four builds. Make them pick the first one.

**Write the cut-down version on the card yourself.** The card is the contract. At 3:00
when someone's wandering, you point at their card.

### 1:35–2:25 — build block one
Circulate constantly. Sit down at each table for 3–4 minutes, twice.

Watch for the two failure modes:
- **Tool shopping.** Someone with six tabs open comparing platforms. Kill it: "Pick the
  one you already pay for. Today is not the day to evaluate tools."
- **Silent stuck.** Someone who's stopped typing and won't say anything. Go to them.
  They will not raise a hand.

Start the **stuck list** on the wall — anything you solve twice goes up there so you
stop solving it a third time.

At 2:15 call: **"Ten minutes left in block one. Where are you?"**

### 2:25–2:35 — break
Real break, ten minutes. Say the time out loud and hold it.
Use it to check the three people you're most worried about finishing.

### 2:35–3:25 — build block two
Open by saying the hard rule again: **"By 3:25 it runs end to end. Ugly is fine.
Unfinished is not."**

At 3:05: *"Twenty minutes. If you're not sure yours will run, cut a step out now."*
At 3:15: *"Ten minutes. Stop adding. Make what you have work."*

This is where you're most useful. Anyone who's still building at 3:15 needs you to
help them cut, not to help them add.

### 3:25–3:50 — demos
**The event.**

Everyone stands up, 90 seconds. Use the timer visibly. Format:
1. What was broken
2. Run it — actually run it, live
3. What you'd do next

Rules:
- Broken demos still count. "Here's where it breaks" is a real demo and takes the
  shame out of the room. Applaud those harder.
- You keep time strictly. 20 people × 90 seconds = 30 minutes with no slack.
- If the room is bigger than 15, split demos by table and take the top two per table
  to the front.

### 3:50–4:00 — keep it alive
Three things, no slides:

1. **What breaks first.** API keys expire, free tiers cap, someone changes a form field.
   Whatever they built will break within three weeks. Say it now so they aren't
   surprised and quietly quit.
2. **Run it once before Monday.** The builds that die are the ones nobody touches for
   ten days. One line in their phone's reminders, right now, in the room.
3. **How to hand it to your team.** Write down what it does, what it touches, and who
   to call when it breaks. Five sentences in a doc.

Then: *"If you want help with the next one, my number's on the card. I'll be here
until 4:30."*

**No pitch from the front.** You've had their attention for three hours and they watched
you build. The people who want to work with you will find you at 4:01. Selling from the
stage at 3:58 costs you more than it makes.

### 4:00–4:30 — stay
This is where the business happens. Don't pack up fast.

## If things go wrong

**Only 6 people show.** Good. Run it exactly the same, single table, and you'll ship
three great builds and make six real relationships. Do not apologize for the size —
the moment you call it small, it's small. "Small room, more help each" and move on.

**Somebody wants to watch, not build.** "There are no observers — but pull up to a table
and pick something small, I'll get you through it." If they insist on spectating, let
them, but don't spend your time there.

**Venue wifi dies.** Hotspot yourself first, then the tables that need it most. The
proposal generator and the money analyst builds need almost no bandwidth — move people
onto those.

**You're behind at 3:00.** Cut your own circulating, not the demos. Move demos to 3:20
and shorten to 60 seconds. Never cancel them.

**Somebody dominates.** Give them a job: "You've got this — go help table two with
their webhook." Turns your problem into your co-facilitator.

**A build is genuinely impossible** (no API access, platform locked down). Say so
immediately, don't let them burn 50 minutes. Move them to the closest thing that works
and get them a real win instead of a real failure.
